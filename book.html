The Grass That Breathes Carbon

A book about the carbon hidden in seagrass meadows worldwide, and how one country is learning to measure it from space.

About the book

Seagrass meadows grow along coastlines on every continent except Antarctica, quietly storing carbon that most climate accounting still overlooks. Measuring that carbon at a country scale is a challenge shared by every nation with a seagrass coastline, and very few have found a practical way to do it.

This book tells the story of one attempt to solve that problem, using Indonesia, home to one of the world's largest shares of seagrass, as a detailed working example. It follows the science from scattered field surveys to a working satellite model, including the surprises, dead ends, and open questions along the way. The lessons are built in Indonesia, but built to travel.

Chapters

  1. The Quietest Carbon Sink on Earth
    Seagrass meadows grow on every continent's coastline except Antarctica, quietly storing carbon that most climate accounting still overlooks.
  2. Carbon Hiding in Grass
    Why a patch of underwater grass can hold more climate power than its size suggests.
  3. A Problem No Country Has Fully Solved
    You cannot count what you cannot see, a challenge shared by every nation with a seagrass coastline. Indonesia decided to try anyway.
  4. Stitching a Scattered Puzzle
    Years of field surveys, scattered across a thousand islands, waiting to be pieced into one picture.
  5. Teaching a Satellite to See Seagrass
    How a machine learns to spot a plant it has never touched, and where it still gets it wrong.
  6. Two Rulers, Two Different Answers
    Two honest ways of measuring the same meadow gave two very different numbers, a lesson for anyone building a carbon inventory anywhere.
  7. The Honest Limits of a Model
    Every model has a blind spot, and this chapter looks at seagrass carbon's without flinching.
  8. From One Country to the World
    What Indonesia's map means for the dozens of other nations trying to count their own blue carbon.
  9. An Invitation
    The map is not finished anywhere, and it never fully will be. Here is how anyone, in any coastal country, can help write the next page.
